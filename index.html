Free self-check

Is your trust actually funded?

A trust only protects what's titled in its name. Grab your paperwork and answer four questions.

1

On your most recent recorded house deed, does the owner read like "Jane Doe, Trustee of the Doe Family Trust dated…"? If it just says your name, the house may not be in the trust.

2

Are your bank and brokerage accounts titled to the trust, or do they have a payable-on-death beneficiary?

3

Have you checked the beneficiaries on your IRAs, 401(k)s and life insurance in the last three years?

4

Are your successor trustees still the people you'd choose today, and do they know where your documents are?

If you answered "no" or "not sure" to any of these, send me a copy of your deed. I'll show you what it says, for free.

Free guides

Plain-English answers, free

You should understand your own documents. These are the questions families ask me most.

Moving your house into your trust

  1. Find your current recorded deed. The county recorder has a copy.
  2. Sign a new grant deed from you to yourself as trustee.
  3. File the county's change-of-ownership report with it.
  4. Record it at the county recorder. A transfer into your own revocable trust usually has no transfer tax and doesn't reassess your property tax.

You've been named successor trustee. Now what?

  • Order several certified copies of the death certificate.
  • Find the original trust and any amendments.
  • California requires a written notice to heirs and beneficiaries within 60 days.
  • List every account and property before you move any money.
  • Keep trust money separate from your own, always.

When to update your trust

  • A marriage, divorce, birth or death in the family
  • A move to another state
  • A successor trustee who can no longer serve
  • A new house, or the old one sold
  • Changed wishes for charities, pets or health care
